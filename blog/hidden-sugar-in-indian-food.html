The Hidden Sugar in Indian Food (You'd Never Guess)

By SugarBreak • Updated July 2026 • 7 min read

When people decide to cut sugar, they picture giving up the obvious things — gulab jamun, jalebi, that afternoon chocolate. But the sugar that actually derails most people isn't the sugar they can see. It's the sugar hiding in foods they think are fine.

You can be "off sugar" and still be eating 40, 50, 60 grams of it a day without touching a single sweet. Here's where it hides.

1. Your daily chai (the quiet giant)

This is the big one. A single cup of chai with two teaspoons of sugar has roughly 8–10 grams of added sugar. That doesn't sound like much — until you count how many cups you actually drink.

The real math
Four cups of sweet chai a day × ~9g each = about 36 grams of added sugar — from chai alone, before breakfast even counts. That's already near the entire daily limit many health bodies suggest.

You don't have to give up chai. But cutting from two spoons to one, then to half, then to none over a couple of weeks is one of the single highest-impact changes most people can make. Your taste adjusts faster than you'd expect.

2. "Healthy" breakfast cereals and muesli

The box says multigrain, high-fibre, immunity, protein. The label tells a different story. Many breakfast cereals and even "healthy" muesli and granola are among the most sugar-dense products in the average kitchen — sometimes a quarter to a third sugar by weight.

💡 Rule of thumb: if a breakfast product is marketed hard as healthy, turn the box around. The health claims are on the front; the sugar is on the back.

3. Packaged fruit juice and "nectars"

Fruit juice feels virtuous, but most packaged juices, nectars and fruit drinks are effectively sugar-water with flavour. A glass can carry as much sugar as a cola, minus the fibre that whole fruit gives you. "No added sugar" versions are better but still deliver a heavy dose of natural fruit sugar with none of the fibre to slow it down.

Better move
Eat the actual fruit. An orange has fibre that slows the sugar down and fills you up; a glass of orange juice is several oranges' worth of sugar with the fibre stripped out.

4. Biscuits — even the "digestive" ones

Glucose biscuits, cream biscuits, and yes, most "digestive" and "marie" biscuits marketed as light are built on sugar and refined flour. The problem isn't one biscuit — it's that they're designed to be eaten by the fistful with tea, so the dose stacks up fast and invisibly.

5. Flavoured yoghurt, lassi and "health" drinks

Plain dahi is genuinely good for you. But flavoured yoghurt, sweetened lassi, and the malted "health" drinks and protein mixes many families treat as wholesome are frequently loaded with added sugar. A sweetened fruit yoghurt can hold more sugar than a scoop of ice cream.

6. The savoury surprise: sauces, gravies and namkeen

This is the one nobody expects. Sugar isn't just in sweet things:

  • Ketchup is roughly a quarter sugar.
  • Jarred sauces and restaurant gravies — especially makhani, manchurian, and many restaurant-style dishes — often have sugar added to balance the flavour.
  • Some namkeen and packaged snacks carry sugar in their coatings and masalas.

You won't taste it as "sweet" because it's balanced against salt and spice — but your body counts it all the same.

How to catch hidden sugar

Two habits will find almost all of it:

Read the label properly

Look at "sugars" under carbohydrates on the nutrition panel. Then scan the ingredients — sugar hides under many names: sucrose, glucose syrup, fructose, corn syrup, maltose, dextrose, invert syrup, honey, and "concentrated fruit juice." If any of these appear in the first three ingredients, the product is largely sugar.

Or just scan it

Reading every label is tedious, and homemade and restaurant food has no label at all.
